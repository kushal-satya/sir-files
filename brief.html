Brief

Did the roll revision decide Tamil Nadu's election? What the data say

A two-page brief for journalists and the public

Interim findings. Every figure is computed from public records; data and code are open. Nonpartisan.

What happened

The Special Intensive Revision struck 97.4 lakh names from Tamil Nadu's draft roll in December 2025, 15.2 percent of the electorate. 23.3 lakh were added back. Two-thirds of the deletions were recorded as 'shifted or absent', and they were concentrated in cities: Chennai lost 35.6 percent of its roll, the rest of the state 13.8. The old were cut hardest: 42.6 percent of electors aged 80 and over, mostly as dead, against 8.4 percent of those in their twenties.

Was it aimed at anyone?

Across the 234 seats, deletion rates show no detectable link with how strongly a seat had backed the DMK in 2021 or with its Muslim share, and women and men were cut at nearly the same rate. The data compare constituencies, not people, so they cannot show whether particular individuals were singled out within a seat.

Did it change votes?

Seats that lost more names lost more votes between 2021 and 2026, but no more than the same seats were already losing between 2016 and 2021 through death and migration. Net of that trend the change is +0.02 votes per deleted name, and the data rule out differential losses, beyond what the same seats were already losing, of more than about a quarter of a vote per name; if the deleted had all been voting, each would have taken about three-quarters of a vote with them. The DMK front's own votes did not fall with deletions.

Kolathur

Stalin lost by 8,795 votes after 1,03,812 deletions. Booths that lost more names did not turn against the DMK more than others. A statistical twin built from comparable seats, cut about 30 percent rather than 36, swung by almost the same amount, though that comparison is too coarse to resolve a margin of this size. For the deletions to have reversed the result, the votes they removed would have had to be 65 to 91 percent DMK. For comparison, the DMK won 75 percent of the two-party vote in Kolathur in 2021 and 47 percent in 2026. The evidence leans against a decisive effect, but the tests are not sharp enough to rule one out at this margin.

What it cannot say

Whether eligible people were wrongly removed one by one inside a booth needs the name-level deletion and restoration lists, which sit behind a CAPTCHA. Whether the revision shifted every seat by the same amount cannot be tested without an untreated comparison. In a few seats decided by a handful of votes, including Tiruppattur, decided by one, no single cause can be excluded.

Bottom line

The claim that the revision engineered the statewide result is not supported by the official record. For Kolathur the record neither confirms nor excludes it. Every number here comes from public records through open code; no voter's name, age or address is published.

An independent analysis of public data, not affiliated with any party or with the Election Commission. It publishes no individual voter's name, age or address. Corrections are welcome.
