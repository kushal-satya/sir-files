How this was done

Method and data

What each number rests on, what was assumed, and what we chose not to do. The full report and the two-page brief carry the complete results.

Principles

Every figure on these pages is produced by a script from a public record, and the pages read their numbers from files those scripts write. Nothing is typed in by hand. We publish only aggregates: no voter's name, age, house number or EPIC number appears anywhere, and the claims-and-objections lists were counted, not read for names. A null result is a result. Where a test cannot be run, the page says so rather than substituting a weaker one.

Deletion rate

The pre-revision roll is the draft roll plus the deletions at the draft stage, so the rate is deletions divided by that sum. Women's and men's cuts are measured from January 2025 to the draft, the closest gendered pair available; because that span also includes the year's additions, it slightly understates gross deletions.

Census figures for constituencies

The Census reports villages, towns and sub-districts, not assembly seats. We aggregate SHRUG's shrid-level abstract to the 2008 constituencies with SHRUG's own key, which weights each fragment of a shrid by its share of population or area. Religion is not in SHRUG. A town shrid takes its town's religion shares from the Census religion table; a rural shrid takes its sub-district's rural shares.

The key leaves about a tenth of the state's population unassigned: the big municipal corporations that span several seats, including half of Chennai. We pool that population within each census district and share it among the district's seats in proportion to each seat's shortfall, the gap between its mapped population and what its roll implies at the district's ratio of electors to people. The reconstructed state total is within 0.01 per cent of the Census figure. The cost is that city seats inherit city averages, which hides real differences between, for example, Chennai's Harbour and Mylapore. Every regression is therefore repeated on the seats where under a quarter of the population came this way.

Correlates of deletion

The deletion rate is regressed on eight standardised Census characteristics with district fixed effects, so that each seat is compared with others in its district, and standard errors clustered by district. The same specification adds the DMK front's 2021 vote share to test for partisan targeting. These are associations between constituencies. They do not say that a given kind of person was more likely to be deleted.

The margin bound

If a share w of a seat's D deletions were wrongful and a share t of those people would have voted, the losing front candidate needs a two-party share p among them large enough that their net votes cover the margin M:

p = ½ × (1 + M / (D × w × t))

The seat pages fix the lean at 60:40 and turnout at 65 per cent, and report the number of wrongful deletions this implies, M / (t × (2p − 1)). Case seats are every seat the front lost as runner-up where p is under 55 per cent at w = 0.5, t = 0.65, plus Kolathur. The rule was fixed before any seat-level evidence was looked at.

Forensic checks

On booth returns we run last-digit uniformity tests on the two leading candidates. These are screens, with known false positives in election data (Deckert, Myagkov and Ordeshook 2011), and are read against the number expected by chance across all tests run. On constituency totals we also run the turnout and winner-share diagnostics of Klimek et al. (2012). Namesakes are independents whose names, after initials and titles are removed, match a leading candidate's at a fuzzy-match score of 85 or more.

What we did not do

The booth-level deletion lists sit behind a CAPTCHA on the CEO's portal, and the ECI's voter services block automated requests. We did not attempt to defeat either. Those lists are being collected by hand by a person typing each CAPTCHA, and until they are complete the booth-level test of whether deletions moved the result cannot be run from official files. We used no third-party mirrors of voter lists of unknown provenance.
